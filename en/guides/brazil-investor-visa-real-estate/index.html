The Brazil Investor Visa Through Real Estate: RN 36 in Practice

Buying any property does not, by itself, create a right to residence. The investment has to meet the rule's specific conditions — and the purchase, the financing and the immigration filing have to be coordinated rather than treated as three separate errands.

Brazil offers a specific residence pathway for foreigners who invest in qualifying urban real estate, established by RN 36/2018 and consolidated through later amendments.

The single most important thing to understand about it: buying any property does not, by itself, create a right to residence. The investment has to meet the rule’s specific conditions, and the residence application is a separate filing that has to be built on top of a correctly structured purchase.

The conditions, precisely

RequirementCurrent standard
Minimum investment (most regions)R$1,000,000 in urban real estate
Minimum investment (North & Northeast)R$700,000
Property status acceptedBuilt property, or property under construction with a registered purchase-and-sale agreement
Funds originOwn resources transferred from abroad, evidenced by a bank declaration
Minimum stay in BrazilAt least 14 days within each two-year period, to maintain the authorisation
Initial residence termUp to 2 years, extendable, eventually convertible to indefinite residence if conditions are maintained

Two conditions do most of the damage when they are misread:

Co-ownership is accepted, provided each co-owner independently meets the minimum. A couple buying one qualifying property does not automatically produce two qualifying investors.

Financing is permitted only for the portion of the price above the required minimum. The qualifying amount itself must come from the investor’s own funds. A mortgage that eats into the threshold does not simply reduce the qualifying figure — it can disqualify the structure.

Before you pay

The cheapest part of a property transaction is the document review. The expensive part is discovering a problem after the money has already moved — and an RN 36 purchase carries both the ordinary title risk and the immigration risk on top of it.

Why the funds trail matters more here than anywhere else

The rule requires own resources transferred from abroad, evidenced by a bank declaration. That is not a formality. It sets the entire structure of how you should move money:

  • Transfer in a way that produces a clean, documented trail from your foreign account to the Brazilian transaction.
  • Keep documentation showing the origin of the funds — banks and, indirectly, tax authorities may request it.
  • Do not move large sums informally, through third parties, or in a sequence that is hard to reconstruct later.

Receiving foreign money in Brazil is not automatically tax-neutral either. The treatment depends on the nature of the funds and your residence status at the time. See the tax residency guide →

Diligence on the property itself

RN 36 status does not exempt you from the ordinary discipline of a Brazilian property purchase. Before transferring significant funds:

  • Confirm ownership and review the matrícula (registry record) and the title chain.
  • Check for liens, outstanding taxes and condominium debts.
  • Confirm the seller’s authority to sell.
  • For property under construction, confirm the purchase-and-sale agreement is properly registered — the rule accepts it, but only in registered form.

A property being advertised online does not mean it is legally ready to be sold to you. See the full property purchase guide →

Structure before acquisition

Immigration is not an investment thesis, and an investment is not an immigration strategy. Keeping the two questions separate prevents expensive decisions.

The order that works:

  1. Define the objective. Residence, yield, capital appreciation, family use — these lead to different structures.
  2. Compare holding structures. Personal ownership, a Brazilian holding company, or another entity each carry different capital gains, rental income and repatriation consequences.
  3. Model the tax outcome before closing, not after — including what happens when you eventually sell or repatriate.
  4. Run title diligence on the specific property.
  5. Coordinate the funds transfer so the bank declaration says what RN 36 needs it to say.
  6. File the residence application on the completed, documented structure.

Doing step 6 first, or steps 4 and 5 in the wrong order, is how investors end up owning a good property and holding no usable immigration position.

A question worth asking

If the immigration status were removed from this deal tomorrow, would you still want to own this property at this price? If the answer is no, the investment is carrying the visa rather than the other way round — and that is a structure worth re-examining before funds move.

The exit problem

Selling the property before converting the authorisation to indefinite residence can jeopardise the immigration status tied to it.

This is the clause that most often surprises investors two or three years in, when circumstances change and the property becomes the obvious thing to liquidate. If the property is central to your immigration plan, its sale is an immigration decision, not just a financial one.

Common mistakes on this pathway

  • Assuming any purchase above the threshold qualifies.
  • Splitting one property between co-owners and expecting two qualifying investors.
  • Financing into the qualifying minimum.
  • Moving funds before the documentation trail is designed.
  • Skipping title diligence because the immigration angle felt like the hard part.
  • Selling before conversion to indefinite residence.
  • Confusing a CNPJ or a corporate structure with an immigration solution.

When this needs individualised counsel

Essentially always — this is the pathway where the cost of a wrong assumption is highest. Specifically when:

  • The property is under construction or held through an entity.
  • Funds come from more than one country or more than one source.
  • You intend to rent the property out.
  • Family members need status alongside yours.
  • You may want to sell within the first few years.
